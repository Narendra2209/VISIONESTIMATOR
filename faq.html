Estimating basics

Construction estimating is the process of measuring the work shown in drawings and specifications and calculating the likely cost of materials, labour, plant and other project costs. A good estimate gives contractors, subcontractors and builders a clear basis for quoting, tendering and budgeting.

A quantity takeoff is the measurement of lengths, areas, volumes and counts from construction drawings — for example square metres of roof sheeting or wall cladding, cubic metres of concrete, metres of pipe or the number of light fittings. Takeoffs form the foundation of every estimate and BOQ.

A Bill of Quantities (BOQ) lists each item of work with a description, unit and measured quantity. It allows contractors and subcontractors to price the same scope consistently and is widely used in tendering.

Working with us

Usually the drawings for the trades you are pricing — architectural and structural drawings, plus services drawings (electrical, plumbing or mechanical) where relevant — together with specifications and schedules, the scope or trade packages you need estimated, and any tender documents or addenda. If you have preferred rates, templates or a deadline, include those too. For civil and earthworks jobs, also send survey data and any geotechnical reports. If something is missing, we will let you know. The estimate request form accepts up to 10 files (10 MB in total); for larger drawing sets, include a download link in your message.

Standard estimates are typically delivered within 48 hours. Larger or more complex projects take longer — after reviewing your drawings and scope we confirm the delivery date before work begins, and urgent tender deadlines can be discussed up front.
